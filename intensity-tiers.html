TFF oral-care assessment framework

Alex shared this with Ross, Nelly & Georgia (Feb 25) after Nelly asked for TFF's holistic lexicon. Full descriptor lists were in the email; this is the script they use for mouthwash.

Flavor / aroma (before & start) — What stands out first? Mint types? Medicinal notes? Sweetness?
First impression (first few seconds) — Burn level? Cooling speed? Sweet vs bitter upfront?
Mid experience — Layered or flat? Medicinal dominate? Balance?
Finish / aftertaste — Clean or lingering? Bitter? Sweet? Film or residue?
Mouthfeel — Watery, sticky, slimy, drying, coating?
Emotional takeaway — Clinical? Gentle? Premium? Chemical? Fresh? Confident?

Dan's Listerine notes map cleanly to this framework — useful language for July 8 tasting and competitive benchmarking.

Workstream B · GA-22131 Garlic Mint functional flavor

Parallel track on the same email chain. Ross shipped samples to Princeton (3 sets for Nelly, Georgia, Ross). Ryan answered Nelly's efficacy question with structured hypothesis.

"Bear with me while I take a mad scientist approach to this…" — Ryan Wixted · Feb 25, 2026 · to Nelly
1

Source material

GA-22131 garlic component from alcohol-free garlic extract — not crushed raw clove. Parallels trending homemade garlic rinses but formulated for commercial mouthwash.

2

Active compounds

Allicin and diallyl disulfide (DADS) — main drivers of antifungal/antibacterial effects in garlic rinse literature.

Dose math (Ryan): 1 g crushed garlic in 100 mL rinse ≈ 0.0005–0.005% allicin + DADS in finished product.
GA-22131 rinses shipped: 0.0004–0.004% — comparable to 1 g clove equivalence.
Ross context: average clove is 3–5 g, so 1 g/use is less objectionable than it sounds.
3

Chlorite stability risk · Ryan flagged early

Organosulfur compounds "could likely fall victim to oxidation by our old friend Sodium Chlorite" — unclear if efficacy or just garlic flavor strength fades. Ross asked for creator-side assessment Mar 2; Ryan committed to check.

Ross's sensory read · sample in hand

Aroma

More off-putting and unusual for mouthwash than taste. Garlic plays into aroma more than flavor.

Taste & heat

No raw-garlic burn/heat — appropriate for TheraBreath. Pleasantly surprised overall.
