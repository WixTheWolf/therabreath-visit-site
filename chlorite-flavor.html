TheraBreath OXYD-8 · stabilized chlorite

Flavor in an oxidative oral-care base

TheraBreath's rinse is clear, aqueous, and pH-neutral — but stabilized chlorite systems are chemically active. Building flavor that stays true over shelf life takes a different playbook than standard mouthwash or beverage work.

Why this matters

This is oxidation chemistry, not a taste preference. Some flavor families that work beautifully in other bases can fade, shift, or develop off-notes in chlorite within weeks.

At a high level
  • Stabilized chlorite systems maintain active oxidative species — they interact with certain flavor compound classes
  • Flavor oils must be properly incorporated into the aqueous base — process and order matter
  • The practical pH window for consumer rinses is narrow — stability and taste both depend on staying in range
  • Goal: acceptable taste with no browning, haze, or harsh chemical character over shelf life

How TFF approaches it

  • Formulate inside your base chemistry — not around it
  • Build flavor systems for emulsification and clarity before aqueous addition
  • Use stability-appropriate character builders rather than fragile single-note dependencies
  • Run structured retain testing at refrigerated, ambient, and accelerated conditions
  • Validate every workshop prototype and pipeline concept before it reaches a tasting room
